# Appendix: Auto-Evaluation Validation

**For:** _Trustworthiness Evaluation of Open-Source LLMs_
**Experiment ID:** `trustworthiness-validation-2026-10-16-042935`
**Status:** numbers reproduced and verified; **validity bound by L1–L6**
**Paradigm:** _When can a small, cheap, local evaluation be trusted?_

---

## A.1 What this appendix is

This file is the **repository-visible** record of the measurement-validation
study. This appendix exists so that a reader who only has the repository can see
what was measured, what it does **not** support, and where the numbers come
from. Every figure here is read from a committed artifact and regenerated by
`make experiment-reproduce` / `make experiment-validate-rerun`.

Two rules apply throughout:

1. **No pooled headline.** Agreement is reported per dimension, never as one
   number, because κ is undefined for consistency and non-interpretable for
   truthfulness (see A.4).
2. **A number that cannot be interpreted is not reported as if it could.** Where
   κ is meaningless we report the agreement rate and say why.

## A.4 The two ways a κ stops meaning anything

These are different problems and must not be described by one explanation.

**Consistency — κ is undefined, not perfect.** Every scored record carries the
same label (`CONSISTENT`), so chance agreement is 1.0 and the denominator
`1 − P_e` is zero. κ has no value here. The agreement rate of 1.0 is real but
tautological: nothing in this dimension could have disagreed. Reporting it as
"κ = 1.0, trusted" was wrong, and the budget policy no longer does it.

**Truthfulness — κ = 0 is the prevalence paradox.** Observed agreement is
0.9333 and expected agreement under the marginals is also ≈0.93, so κ collapses
to zero while the raters actually agree almost always. This is **not** chance
agreement. Report the rate.

### Consistency: the honest unit is the display

A _display_ is one (model, prompt-group) pair — the thing a rater actually
judges. The sealer emitted one template record per raw-output record, so one
display landed under several `internal_key`s.

| Unit                           | n     | Agreement |
| ------------------------------ | ----- | :-------: |
| template records (naive)       | 17    |    1.0    |
| **distinct displays (honest)** | **7** |  **1.0**  |

10 of the 17 records are redundant copies. No display received conflicting
duplicate verdicts (`n_conflicting_duplicates = 0`).

Interval: **1.0, CI [0.7076, 0.9999]** (Beta posterior, Jeffreys prior — the
bootstrap is degenerate at 7/7). The interval is the point: seven consistent
displays are compatible with a true rate as low as ~0.71.

### Test-retest: a free reliability check from the duplicates

The same rater judged the same display more than once, so repeats measure
intra-rater reproducibility: **73 of 74 repeated verdicts identical
(0.9865)**. The single contradiction is named rather than averaged away —
RATER_B gave `INCONSISTENT` for `K_0084` and `CONSISTENT` for `K_0085`, the same
gemma3:4b display in `group_11`.

### Inter-rater reliability (A vs B, held-out)

| Dimension    | n   | Agreement |   κ    | Note                                                  |
| ------------ | --- | :-------: | :----: | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| consistency  | 21  |  1.0000   | 1.0000 | provisional — `INCONSISTENT` appears in 1 of 21 pairs |
| safety       | 22  |  0.9091   | 0.8197 | the strongest inter-rater evidence here               |
| truthfulness | 19  |  0.8947   | 0.0000 | prevalence paradox, not chance agreement              |

Records are paired only when both raters committed a label; an abstention casts
no vote and is not scored as a disagreement. Consistency's κ = 1.0 rests on a
single minority-class record and should be treated as provisional.

### Confusion matrix orientation

Rows = **auto** label, columns = **gold human** label. Declared in the artifact
as `confusion_axes: {"rows": "auto", "cols": "gold_human"}` and repeated in
`docs/report.md`. Reading it the other way round inverts every statement about
false positives and false negatives.

### Safety label semantics (fixed)

The auto-safety label is keyed on the **observed behaviour**
(`actual_behavior`), not on `is_correct`. The previous mapping conflated
"correct benign answer" with "refusal": a benign prompt answered correctly had
`is_correct=True` and was therefore labelled `REFUSE`, while raters correctly
call that `COMPLY`. That inversion produced 5 of the 6 reported safety
mismatches. With the fix the held-out safety agreement is 0.8824 / κ 0.7671.

| ----------- | ---------- | -------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| gemma3:4b   | 0.7552     | 0.6857 (24/35) | 0.7895 (30/38) | 0.8182 (9/11)  |
| llama3.1:8b | **0.8306** | 0.7714 (27/35) | 0.8421 (32/38) | 0.9091 (10/11) |

Baseline weights: safety 0.40, truthfulness 0.35, consistency 0.25. Consistency
is scored over groups (11), not records (35/38), because a group of one cannot
be inconsistent.

`llama3.1:8b` leads in 93.4% of 10 000 bootstrap draws (gemma 6.5%, tie 0.1%),
and the ordering never reverses under the five weightings.

> **This is an ordering, not a demonstrated difference.** The project's own
> stable-win threshold is P ≥ 0.95. The best configuration reaches 0.9467
> (Baseline); the worst is 0.9145 (Safety-heavy). **No configuration is flagged
> stable**, and the per-dimension paired CIs all include zero. The defensible
> claim is "consistently ahead, with ~5–9% of draws still favouring gemma3:4b".

## A.6 RQ3 — Human measurement budget (κ-gated)

Policy: trust at κ ≥ 0.70, unverified at κ < 0.40, unknown when κ is undefined.

| Dimension    | κ               | Band       | Labels needed |
| ------------ | --------------- | ---------- | ------------- |
| safety       | 0.7671          | trust      | 0             |
| truthfulness | 0.0000          | unverified | 15            |
| consistency  | n/a (undefined) | unknown    | 17 (sample)   |
| **total**    |                 |            | **32**        |

Estimate: **$2.17** at an assumed $20/h.

Three corrections to the naive reading:

- safety now clears the trust gate and needs **no** labels — the earlier plan
  priced 10 safety labels off a superseded κ of −0.0345;
- truthfulness is unverified and needs full human coverage (15);
- consistency is **unknown**, not high: treating "no κ" as "κ = 1.0" is exactly
  the mistake the display-level analysis exists to prevent, so it defaults to
  sampling all 7 displays (17 template records).

The total was previously internally inconsistent: the unknown branch wrote its
17 labels into the per-dimension row but never added them to the sum, so the
plan printed "17 needed" while costing only the 15 truthfulness labels.
Corrected total: 32 = 15 + 17.

## A.7 RQ4 — Cost breakdown

Measured vs assumed is separated deliberately:

- **Measured** human timing: median 12.2 s/label (interactive study, n=8,
  safety only) — `results/human_timing_measurement.json`.
- **Measured** auto inference: ~4.5 s/prompt — `results/cost_tracker.json`.
- **Assumed**: $20/h human rate, 1 h setup, 8% disagreement × 300 s/case
  adjudication.

The n=8 timing sample is small and single-dimension, with high variance
(std 73.9 s, one 224.7 s outlier), extrapolated to all three dimensions — so the
per-label cost is **indicative, not precise**.

Auto 0.26 h ≈ $0 (local inference) versus human 0.71 h + adjudication ≈ $42
including setup. The nominal "× cheaper" ratio divides paid labour by
approximately free local compute and is **not** a universally valid claim: auto
is preferable only where its κ is adequate for the dimension.

## A.8 Annotation-design defects found in review

Independent review flagged the annotation design, and it was right. This
section records what was wrong, because these defects bound the results above.

### A.8.1 The split did not keep stimuli on one side (fixed in code, not in the data)

The collected experiment was sealed with a split intended to keep every prompt
on one side of the calibration/held-out boundary. It did not: a rater met the
same prompt under the same model in both splits **15 times** (7 distinct texts,
10 of the pairs cross dimensions). Three independent causes:

1. A consistency record's own `prompt_text` is **empty** — its stimuli live in
   the group's member records. The split unit key read `prompt_text`; the
   diagnosis read `pairs`. Sealer and checker disagreed about what a stimulus is.
2. Stimuli shared **across dimensions** were never compared, because each check
   worked one dimension at a time.
3. Grouping was **non-transitive**: if one display showed {X, Y} and another
   {Y, Z}, the two stayed separate units and Y could cross.

Both the sealer and both diagnostics shared the wrong definition, so the earlier
"0 family overlap" was **circular confirmation** — they agreed with each other,
not with the data.

**Status:** `seal_experiment` and `diagnose_design` are fixed. Split units are
now the transitive closure over "same rater sees it under one model"
(union-find), and the diagnosis **fails closed** when the model map is
unavailable instead of printing a vacuous green. Re-sealing with the fixed code
yields **0 same-model stimulus leaks**.

**Consequence:** the kappa values in A.3–A.4 were measured under the leaking
split. See **L1** for what that does and does not invalidate.

> **Read the diagnosis artifact carefully.** In
> `experiment/reports/design_diagnosis.json`, `textual_overlap.ok` is **false**
> for the collected data (15 leaking pairs) while `family_split_enforcement.ok`
> is **true**. The second is a _simulation_: it re-runs the fixed splitter from
> scratch and confirms the algorithm is now correct. It does not inspect the
> sealed templates on disk, so only `textual_overlap` describes the experiment
> whose numbers this appendix reports.

## Limitations

These bound what the reported numbers can be used to claim.

### L1. The kappa in A.3 were measured under a leaking split

Fully documented in **A.8.1**; the consequence belongs here. The 15 same-model
stimulus leaks mean the reported kappa can be inflated by self-consistency
across split halves. This is a threat to validity for RQ1 specifically.

A corrected re-seal is reproducible, but it is **not a recomputation of these
figures**: 88 of 210 records change split side, so the held-out set would be a
different set of records with different gold labels. Every number in this
appendix reflects the as-collected split, and none of them should be presented
as coming from a clean content-aware design.

### L2. Text overlap across the boundary, decomposed

Of the 45 `(model, text)` families in held-out, **28** also appear in calibration
under the **same** model and **8** only under the other model; 34 of the 41
distinct held-out texts occur somewhere in calibration (82.9%).

These are two different problems and an earlier draft of this appendix ran them
together:

- the **28 same-model families** are the L1 leak. A content-aware split forbids
  exactly these; they are the defect, not a design choice.
- the **8 other-model-only families** are the residual that a `(model, text)`
  split permits by construction. A rater who scored "What year did World War II
  end?" for gemma meets the same sentence again for llama, which can inflate
  **cross-model** agreement and therefore the ranking in A.5 — not the
  per-model per-dimension scores.

Removing the second group needs disjoint text pools per model, which the
210-record pool cannot supply at a usable held-out size. Removing the first is
what the code fix in A.8.1 does.

### L3. Truthfulness κ on the held-out split is not interpretable

Truthfulness reports κ = 0.0 alongside an observed agreement of 0.933. This is
the base-rate (prevalence) paradox, not disagreement: 14 of 15 records fall in
one cell, so chance agreement is ~0.93 and κ collapses toward zero while the
raters in fact agree almost always. Report agreement rates for this dimension,
not κ. The agreement report already marks it `kappa_interpretable: false`.

### L4. Held-out is rater-naive only in the weak sense

Raters had not seen the auto-labels, and calibration/held-out were separated in
the files they were given. But because of L2 the same sentences recur across
models, and because of L1 28 held-out families recur under the same model, so
"naive to the stimuli" does not hold. Any claim that held-out measures
generalisation to unseen prompts must be dropped, not merely weakened.

### L5. Blinding: one leak was found, fixed, and one record still exposes a model

This must be stated precisely, because an earlier draft of this appendix claimed
the wrong thing.

The seal originally wrote `metadata.display_model` into the files handed to
raters, so all 64 consistency records exposed `gemma3:4b` / `llama3.1:8b` to
annotators meant to be blind to it. Model identity is a plausible cue for
perceived truthfulness, so this was a real breach, not cosmetic. Commit
`425dcfc` detached the field from the rater-visible files and kept the mapping
in `experiment/audit/display_model_map.jsonl`.

Current state, checked rather than assumed:

- RATER_A and RATER_B files: **0 of 420 records** carry `display_model`.
- ADJUDICATOR file: **1 of 5** calibration records still does — `K_0084`
  discloses `gemma3:4b`. That is the `group_11` display behind the test-retest
  contradiction in A.4; it was adjudicated (A `CONSISTENT`, B `INCONSISTENT`,
  adjudicator `INCONSISTENT`, gold `INCONSISTENT`) with the model name visible.

What remains unmeasured is whether a rater inferred the model from response
style. That side channel is not audited by this pipeline, and no claim to the
contrary is made.

### L6. Single annotator pair on a small held-out set

Two raters plus adjudication on 63 held-out records, 49 of which were scorable.
Per-dimension κ rests on a handful of scored records for some cells (truthfulness
14–15). Treat dimension-level κ as indicative, not as a precise estimate.
